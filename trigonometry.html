The cases

Eight places trigonometry does the heavy lifting

Every panel is drawn live on a canvas. Move your cursor over the interactive sims, drag the sliders on the others, and pause the whole page up top.

Case 01 · Core math 01

One circle explains every rotation and wave

Spin a radius around a circle. Its shadow on the x-axis is cosine; its height is sine. Track that point over time and the same motion unrolls into the two waves every game engine leans on. Master this pair and the next seven cases are variations on a theme.

x = cos θ · y = sin θ · sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 Auto-rotating
Case 02 · Aiming 02

atan2 turns a target into an angle

A turret only needs to know which way to point. Pass the horizontal and vertical gap to atan2 and it returns the angle — correct in every quadrant, even when the target is directly above. Feed that angle to a cosine and sine to get the barrel direction.

θ = atan2(Δy, Δx) ↖ Move cursor
Case 03 · Projectiles 03

A cannon shot is a triangle with gravity

Launch speed splits into two independent motions: horizontal v·cosθ and vertical v·sinθ. Gravity only touches the vertical one, so the path is a parabola. Tune the angle and power to drop the shot on the target — and watch the range curve peak.

vx = v cos θ · vy = v sin θ − gt Adjust the sliders
Case 04 · Orbits 04

Orbiting patrols are cosine plus sine

Patrol routes, orbiting cameras, pickup halos and rotating shields all place objects with cosine and sine. Feed both the same angle and you get a point on a circle. Their two waves are permanently 90° out of phase — one peaks while the other is at zero.

x = r cos θ · y = r sin θ Auto-orbiting
Case 05 · Stealth 05

Vision cones are a dot product in disguise

Stealth games don't need an expensive raycast to know if you're seen. Compare the guard's facing vector with the vector to the player: when cos θ is large enough and you're inside the range, you're spotted. Sweep the cone and watch the threshold flip.

cos θ = (a · b) / (|a||b|) ↖ Move cursor
Case 06 · IK 06

Reaching limbs are solved triangles

Two-bone IK — arms, legs, spider mechs, ropes — is the law of cosines at work. One cosine gives the elbow's interior angle; atan2 aims the whole limb. Clamp the target distance and the limb can never stretch out of its socket.

c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C ↖ Move cursor
Case 07 · Organic motion 07

“Alive” is a sine wave on top of the pose

Idle breathing, floating pickups, flags, seaweed, camera bob: all the small organic details are sine waves layered over a base position. Amplitude controls how far it drifts; frequency controls how fast. Drag both and watch the curve rebuild.

y(t) = A sin(ωt + φ) Adjust the sliders
Case 08 · Homing 08

Homing missiles chase an angle, not a point

A missile can't teleport its nose at the target. Each frame it measures the bearing with atan2, takes the shortest angle difference, and turns by at most its turn rate. Too slow and it overshoots; too fast and it feels magnetic.

Δθ = wrap(θ_target − θ_heading) Auto-chase

Pocket reference

The formulas behind the demos

Eight lines that cover most gameplay math. Everything else is a variation or a combination.

Aim θ = atan2(Δy, Δx)

Quadrant-safe angle from a direction vector. No special cases.

Launch vx = v cos θ · vy = v sin θ

Split a diagonal speed into independent horizontal and vertical parts.

Rotation x' = x cos θ − y sin θ
y' = x sin θ + y cos θ

Spin sprites, cameras, and minimaps without losing their shape.

Detection cos θ = (a · b) / (|a||b|)

Compare facing and target direction. Compare the result to a cone threshold.

Reach c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

The law of cosines: find a joint angle from three side lengths.

Looping wrap(θ) = ((θ + π) mod 2π) − π

Shortest angle difference. Essential for smooth turning and steering.

Organic y = A sin(2πft + φ)

Amplitude, frequency and phase — the anatomy of every idle animation.

Identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Why the unit circle stays a circle, and why normalized vectors stay length 1.
